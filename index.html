About

I am an economist by training and an analytics leader by profession. I look at business problems through an economic lens: behaviour, demand and supply, market structure and strategy. Then I build the model, the process and the team that turn that view into a decision.

At HSBC, I built a 25+ member stress-testing team. At Deutsche Bank, I led the Finance Intelligence reporting team. At Hewlett Packard, I created an award-winning commodity-risk analytics programme. At GE Corporate Treasury, I modelled derivatives risk. Before that, I was a Fox International Fellow at Yale University and a Senior Research Fellow at JNU. My research appears in the Journal of Asian Economics and the Revue d'économie financière. Since February 2025, I have worked as a freelance consultant.

Research & publications

Les liens entre la rentabilité des actions et les fondamentaux macroéconomiques en Inde
A. Kanakaraj & B. Karan Singh · Revue d'économie financière, No. 107 (2012), pp. 181–198 · English title: Asset Returns in the Indian Stock Market and its Linkages with Macroeconomic Fundamentals · Read on Cairn
Asks whether India's stock-market boom of the 2000s reflects macroeconomic fundamentals. It finds a strong link between macro performance and equity returns.
Revisiting the empirical existence of the Phillips curve for India
B. Karan Singh, A. Kanakaraj & T. O. Sridevi · Journal of Asian Economics, Vol. 22, Issue 3 (2011), pp. 247–258 · doi:10.1016/j.asieco.2011.01.002
Estimates India's output gap with a Kalman filter. Once supply shocks are included, a Phillips curve clearly exists for India in recent years.
Stock Prices, Micro Reasons and Macro-Economy in India, 1997–2007
A. Kanakaraj · MacMillan Center Working Paper, Yale University, 2007
Fox International Fellowship Alumni Conference — Global Challenges: Interdisciplinary Voices Across the World
University of Cambridge, 15 March 2025 · Paper presented at the conference marking 25 years of the Yale–Cambridge Fox partnership
“Navigating Climate Change Risk: Leveraging NGFS Framework for Effective Decision-Making in Banking Industry”. The paper reviews the NGFS Phase 5 climate scenarios, such as Net Zero, Delayed Transition and Current Policy. It sets out how banks can turn physical and transition risk into economic variables and time horizons. Banks need these for strategic decisions and regulatory stress tests.
