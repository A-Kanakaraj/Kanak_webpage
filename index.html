About

I am an economist by training and an analytics leader by profession. My strength is looking at a business problem through an economic lens — behaviour, demand and supply, market structure, strategy and equilibrium — and then building the model, the process and the team that turn that view into a decision senior management can act on.

Over 16 years in industry I built a 25+ member stress-testing team at HSBC, led the Finance Intelligence management-reporting team at Deutsche Bank, created Hewlett Packard's award-winning commodity-risk analytics programme, and modelled derivatives risk for GE Corporate Treasury. Before that I was a Fox International Fellow at Yale University and a Senior Research Fellow at JNU, with publications in the Journal of Asian Economics and the Revue d'économie financière.

What I'm looking for: a senior role (Director / Head of) in stress testing, capital and risk analytics, treasury analytics or decision science — in banking or in an analytics firm serving banks. Bangalore-based; open to relocation and hybrid arrangements.

Research & publications

Les liens entre la rentabilité des actions et les fondamentaux macroéconomiques en Inde
A. Kanakaraj & B. Karan Singh · Revue d'économie financière, No. 107 (2012), pp. 181–198 · English title: Asset Returns in the Indian Stock Market and its Linkages with Macroeconomic Fundamentals · Read on Cairn
Asks whether India's stock-market boom of the 2000s could be explained by macroeconomic fundamentals, and finds a strong connection between recent macro performance and equity returns.
Revisiting the empirical existence of the Phillips curve for India
B. Karan Singh, A. Kanakaraj & T. O. Sridevi · Journal of Asian Economics, Vol. 22, Issue 3 (2011), pp. 247–258 · doi:10.1016/j.asieco.2011.01.002
Uses a non-linear Kalman filter to estimate India's output gap and shows that, once supply shocks are accounted for, a Phillips curve clearly exists for India in recent years.
